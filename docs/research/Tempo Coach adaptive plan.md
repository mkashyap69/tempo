# Turn Tempo's planner into a day-aware coach

Tempo already has most of a Garmin-Coach-style engine. A pure-Dart weekly planner, recovery-based day states and a once-a-morning adaptation with carry-forward and RPE feedback all exist in `packages/scoring/lib/src/coach.dart`. What it lacks is any sense of time within the day. Sessions have no slot, plan rows have no done/missed status, adaptation is locked to once a day, and the app sends only two static notifications that have no actions ([coach.dart:600-771](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/coach.dart); [notifications.dart:9-115](../../app/lib/src/core/notifications.dart)). The build therefore has four parts, in this order: a completion model, a pure `replanToday` function that turns "missed this morning" into an evening, shorter or easier option bounded by bedtime, one unified Coach screen that also shows the Longevity focus lever's daily actions, and a pre-scheduled, capped, actionable notification layer that fully replaces its pending notifications on every app open, sync and action. The science supports the core rules. Downgrade hard work when readiness drops, which is a binary swap in the HRV-guided trials. Never stack missed sessions. Finish anything hard at least 4 hours before sleep, because bouts ending earlier showed no sleep cost across about 4 million WHOOP nights ([Leota 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58271-x)). The platforms force a "pre-schedule, then cancel and replace" design: coaching apps can't claim `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, iOS keeps only 64 pending notifications, and neither OS runs background work on a schedule. Notification evidence argues for very few pushes. Prompt effects in HeartSteps fell by about 2% a day and were indistinguishable from zero around day 28 ([Klasnja 2019](https://academic.oup.com/abm/article/53/6/573/5091257)). The phased plan at the end turns all of this into schema v8, new settings keys, thresholds, a notification inventory, platform changes, tests and a gate for each phase. Several product choices remain open, and the user has to make them.

**How to read this report.** Claims tagged *Evidence* are backed by the cited source. Claims tagged *Design inference* are this report's proposals and are not proven. Repo facts cite files at commit `d7e3fdf`. Most product and academic sources were only seen as search-engine snippets, because the research proxy blocked full-page reads. Exact wording and numbers from those sources should be treated as "per the page summary".

## Leading products adapt in one of three ways, and none rescues a missed morning session

*Evidence.* Adaptive coaches follow one of three models. The first is stateless daily re-suggestion, used by Garmin Daily Suggested Workouts, Whoop Strain Target, Athlytic and Fitbod. A missed session there "is simply lost and you continue with the workout schedule for the next day" ([Garmin Forums](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-945/251132/daily-suggested-workout-skipping-missing-a-training)). Fitbod "recalibrates future workouts so you don't need to 'catch up'" ([Fitbod](https://fitbod.me/blog/how-fitbod-personalizes-your-workout-plan-using-smart-training-algorithms/)). The second is a plan that rebuilds itself automatically. Garmin Coach "rebuild[s] the schedule daily", swapping a tempo run for an easy one after illness and pushing the hard session out a week ([shoulditrain](https://www.shoulditrain.com/blog/garmin-coach-review)). It also adjusts upward when benchmark runs beat expectations ([gneta](https://www.gneta.app/blog/garmin-coach-review)). The third is a plan the user reschedules. Runna lets you drag a session within ±1 week, and after more than three misses it shows a "Plan Realignment" pop-up offering to skip or rearrange them ([Runna](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10026375-how-to-use-the-plan-realignment-feature); [Runna calendar](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10137793-how-to-use-your-training-calendar)). TrainingPeaks reschedules only manually and grades compliance against plan: within 20% is green, within 50% yellow, beyond that red ([TrainingPeaks guide](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/231472468-TrainingPeaks-Athlete-User-Guide)). Illness is handled through an explicit mode, not silently. Oura's Rest Mode turns off activity goals, and Oura prompts the user to turn it on when body temperature rises ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/ouras-new-rest-mode-helps-optimize-your-recovery/)).

*Evidence.* Within a single day, the research found **no product that moves a missed morning workout to the evening**. Intra-day coaching takes other forms: a strain target that refreshes every 10 minutes, with a haptic when you reach it ([Whoop](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/Strain-Coach?language=en_US)); inactivity prompts after 50 minutes ([Oura](https://help.ouraring.com/activity/inactivity-alerts)) or at :50 past the hour ([iMore](https://www.imore.com/how-customize-notifications-activity-app-apple-watch)); an end-of-day "close your rings" nudge ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/watch/close-your-rings/)); and a wind-down prompt one hour before the suggested bedtime ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/ideal-bedtime/)). User complaints fall into three groups. The first is intrusive coaching: a TechRadar reviewer turned off Workout Buddy ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/i-tried-watchos-26s-workout-buddy-but-had-to-turn-it-off-heres-why)). The second is silent re-targeting: Garmin Coach users saw sudden HR-zone changes with no explanation ([Garmin Forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/402015/garmin-coach-new-adaptive-workouts-sudden-change-of-suggested-hr-zones-in-workouts)). The third is missed work that simply disappears.

*Design inference.* Tempo should borrow from each model. It should re-suggest daily as Garmin DSW does, keep a light weekly skeleton with a "realign?" prompt as Runna does, and keep its existing explicit Pause mode, which plays the role of Oura's Rest Mode. The same-day rescue is a real differentiator, as long as it is bounded by bedtime and always explained. Tempo already writes a one-line reason with a glyph on every plan change ([coach_service.dart:127-207](../../app/lib/src/core/coach_service.dart)). That habit should carry into every notification.

## The science gives binary downgrades, no stacking and a 4-hour bedtime buffer

**Readiness.** *Evidence.* The HRV-guided trials use a simple binary rule. Javaloyes et al. compared a 7-day rolling lnRMSSD with a band of baseline mean ± 0.5 SD. When the value fell outside the band, a moderate or hard session became low intensity or rest. Cyclists improved 40-minute time-trial performance by 7.3% ([Javaloyes 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/325434300_Training_Prescription_Guided_by_Heart_Rate_Variability_in_Cycling)). Vesterinen's HRV group did fewer HIIT sessions and still improved more ([Vesterinen 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295900195_Individual_Endurance_Training_Prescription_with_Heart_Rate_Variability)). Meta-analyses disagree on whether HRV guidance beats a fixed plan on VO2max ([Granero-Gallegos 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/21/7999/htm); [Manresa-Rocamora 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8507742/)). The fair summary is "equal or better, for less hard work". This matters for the Mi Band 6 because **live HR gives no RR intervals** ([docs/decisions.md](../../docs/decisions.md), 2026-10-05 Explorer v2 entry). Tempo's readiness signal is therefore an RHR/HRV *proxy*. The RHR rules in use (≥5 bpm above baseline means downgrade; ≥10% above, or 3 or more days elevated, means rest or check for illness) come from practitioner sources only ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/the-4-signs-of-overtraining/); [Welltory](https://welltory.com/blog/overtraining-hrv-drops-resting-heart-rate)). No trial validates RHR-only or sleep-guided session selection.

**Load.** *Evidence.* Foster monotony (weekly mean daily load ÷ SD) above 2.0 together with high load is a classic warning sign ([Foster](https://science-cycling.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Foster-presentatie-gecomprimeerd.pdf)). Impellizzeri et al. conclude that the acute:chronic workload ratio has no established causal link to injury and shouldn't drive recommendations ([IJSPP 2020](https://journals.humankinetics.com/downloadpdf/journals/ijspp/15/6/article-p907.xml)). In a 532-runner RCT, a <10% weekly ramp did not reduce injuries ([PMC9528699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528699/)). Tempo's current `cardioLoad` uses a 7/28-day ratio with bands from 0.8 to 1.3, and "overreaching" forces a strain cap ([load.dart:1-73](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/load.dart); [coach.dart:535-581](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/coach.dart)). *Design inference:* keep it as a "load trend" display. Add monotony as a soft rule, and don't present the ratio as an injury-risk score.

**Missed sessions and evening sessions.** *Evidence.* Coaching practice is to reschedule key sessions (intervals, long) only when doing so doesn't crowd the next key session, to drop easy sessions, and never to stack missed work ([Be The Fear](https://www.bethefear.com.au/be-the-fear-blog/what-to-do-when-you-miss-a-workout), practitioner). A meta-analysis of 23 studies found evening exercise doesn't impair sleep. The exception is vigorous exercise ending ≤1 hour before bed ([Stutz 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-018-1015-0)). In about 14,689 WHOOP users, bouts ending at least 4 hours before sleep onset showed no sleep change at any strain. Later, harder bouts were linked to later and shorter sleep, higher night RHR and lower HRV ([Leota 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58271-x)). This gives the evening rescue three tiers. With at least 4 hours to bedtime, do the original session. With 1 to 4 hours, do an easier and shorter one. With less than 1 hour, do light movement or nothing.

**Dose floors.** *Evidence.* WHO recommends 150–300 minutes a week of moderate activity plus 2 or more strength days ([WHO 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/)). Strength training's mortality benefit peaks at about 30–60 minutes a week ([Momma 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9209691/)). A 4×4-minute session at 90–95% HRmax raised VO2max by 7.2% ([Helgerud 2007](https://rcc.hslu.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/downloads/sport/Aerobic_High-Intensity_Intervals_Improve_J.Helgerud_2007.pdf)). Step benefits plateau at 6–8k a day for people 60 and over and 8–10k for younger adults ([Paluch 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpub/article/PIIS2468-2667(21)00302-9/fulltext)). Strength coaches deload every 4–8 weeks at −40–60% volume ([Bell 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0)). Tempo's lever targets already match these numbers: 300 active minutes a week, 2 strength sessions a week and 10k/8k steps ([longevity.dart:246-262](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/longevity.dart)). Because "some activity beats none", short minimum-dose fallbacks of 10–20 minutes are better than skipping when the week is behind.

**Adherence.** *Evidence.* If-then implementation intentions have small-to-medium effects on physical activity, d ≈ 0.24–0.31, and work better with barrier planning ([Bélanger-Gravel 2013](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17437199.2011.560095)). In HeartSteps, walking suggestions raised steps over the next 30 minutes by 24%. Anti-sedentary prompts had no detectable effect ([Klasnja 2019](https://academic.oup.com/abm/article/53/6/573/5091257)). *Design inference:* when onboarding Tempo Coach, ask for a training slot as an if-then plan ("after coffee, 07:00"). For the steps lever, nudge with walking suggestions rather than "stand up" alerts.

## The platforms force pre-scheduling and full replacement, not live triggers

*Evidence: Android.* `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is "for calendar and alarm clock apps only". `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is denied by default on Android 14+ and the user has to grant it ([Android 14](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)). Inexact `set()` fires within an hour of the trigger time, and `setWindow()` windows are at least 10 minutes ([Schedule alarms](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule)). While-idle alarms are limited to 7 an hour in Doze. Standby buckets cap alarms at 10/h (Working set), 2/h (Frequent), 1/h (Rare) and **1 a day in Restricted**, which an app reaches after 8 days without use on Android 13+ ([power limits](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details); [standby buckets](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby)). Xiaomi's MIUI/HyperOS also needs the user to turn on Autostart and "No restrictions" ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/xiaomi)). That matters for a Mi Band user, who is quite likely on a Xiaomi phone.

*Evidence: iOS.* iOS keeps the 64 soonest-firing pending local notifications, and a repeating notification counts as one ([Apple Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171)). BGAppRefresh's `earliestBeginDate` is a floor, not a schedule ([Uy Nguyen](https://uynguyen.github.io/2020/09/26/Best-practice-iOS-background-processing-Background-App-Refresh-Task/)). Core Bluetooth state restoration relaunches the app for BLE events with about 10 seconds to act, but not after a force-quit or a Bluetooth toggle ([QA1962](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1962/_index.html); [Core Bluetooth background](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html)). Time-sensitive delivery needs an entitlement and is meant for events "happening now or within an hour" ([UNNotificationInterruptionLevel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel); [WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10091/)). Provisional authorization delivers quietly to Notification Center without a prompt ([WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/710/)).

*Evidence: Flutter.* flutter_local_notifications 22.x offers the `inexactAllowWhileIdle` and `exactAllowWhileIdle` modes. Actions need an `ActionBroadcastReceiver` in the manifest. Background action handlers must be top-level `@pragma('vm:entry-point')` functions, and on iOS they run in a separate isolate that needs `setPluginRegistrantCallback`. An exact schedule without permission logs an error and isn't scheduled ([README](https://github.com/MaikuB/flutter_local_notifications/blob/master/flutter_local_notifications/README.md)). Tempo pins flutter_local_notifications 22.3.1, workmanager 0.10.10 and timezone 0.11.1. Its manifest already has the scheduled and boot receivers but **no `ActionBroadcastReceiver` and no exact-alarm permission** ([AndroidManifest.xml](../../app/android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)). Gradle already sets `minSdk ≥ 26` and core library desugaring ([app/android/app/build.gradle.kts](../../app/android/app/build.gradle.kts)). `openDb()` already uses `shareAcrossIsolates: true`, so a notification-action isolate can open Drift safely ([db.dart](../../app/lib/src/core/db.dart)).

*Design inference.* Treat every background wake as a chance to refine, never as the delivery mechanism. On each re-evaluation, Tempo computes the next 48 hours of notifications from current state as a pure function, cancels its coach IDs and re-issues `zonedSchedule`. Re-evaluations happen on app open or resume, in `afterSync` (WorkManager about every 3 h, foreground sync, pairing), in every notification action, and on time-zone change. Each notification's text bakes in the best estimate at scheduling time, so it reads correctly even if no wake happens before it fires. Where data may be stale, write the text as a question that one of the actions answers. "Got your session in? [Done] [Move to 18:30] [Skip]" works without knowing whether the band synced. **Actions replace missing data.** Deterministic IDs (`1000 + kind*10 + epochDay % 7`) keep a full re-issue idempotent and far below iOS's 64. The Restricted bucket is a genuine risk for a lapsed user. Tempo should accept one notification a day in that state rather than fight the OS.

## Notification UX evidence says two pushes a day, one reason each, and back off when ignored

*Evidence.* One well-timed push works. In the Drink Less trial, a notification raised the odds of opening the app within the next hour 3.5-fold. A bank of 30 novel messages did about as well as a single standard message ([Bell 2023](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e38342)). Volume decays: HeartSteps' day-1 effect of +66% faded at about 2% a day ([Klasnja 2019](https://academic.oup.com/abm/article/53/6/573/5091257)). Personalized HeartSteps treats recent "dose" as a cost and sends only when the expected benefit clears a positive threshold ([Liao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34527853/)). Apple advises against re-sending for the same thing when it is ignored ([HIG](https://developers.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/components/system-experiences/notifications/)). Android's guidance has four relevant rules: no generic engagement pings, importance that matches real urgency, titles of about 30 characters and bodies of about 40, and at most 3 actions in the collapsed view ([Android notifications guide](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/notifications)). Offering a choice raised the effect of a health message more than autonomy-supportive wording alone ([Altendorf 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e14074/)). HeartSteps also treated users as unavailable while walking, running or driving ([Klasnja 2019](https://academic.oup.com/abm/article/53/6/573/5091257)).

*Design inference.* Tempo's default budget is one morning brief plus **at most one** proactive contextual nudge a day. Opted-in event items (wind-down, RPE check-in, health flag) are rate-limited separately, with a hard cap of 4 a day and 14 non-user-requested pushes a week. Follow-ups the user asks for, such as "Remind me at 18:30", don't count against the budget. Copy follows the pattern "[signal] → [meaning] → [one optional action]", always includes a "Not today" option, and avoids "should", "must", "missed" and "don't forget". The band gives Tempo hard availability gates that phone-only apps lack: asleep (from synced sleep), a live session running, and a stale sync, which makes data-dependent nudges unsafe. If a category is ignored 3 times in a row it drops to every other day. After 5 ignores it pauses for 7 days and an in-app card offers to turn it back on. Tapping "Not today" counts as engagement, not as ignoring. These backoff numbers are proposals. **No published thresholds exist** for them.

## The repo already holds about 70% of the engine; the missing seams are precise

*Evidence (repo).* The planner (`weekPlan`), targets (`strainTarget`), day states (`dayState`: rest at recovery ≤33 or overreaching, easeOff below 67 or less than 2 days since a hard session) and `adaptWeek` (a 3-day look-ahead with ≤2 hard sessions a week, at least 2 days apart) are all pure and tested ([coach.dart:431-771](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/coach.dart); [coach_test.dart](../../packages/scoring/test/coach_test.dart)). `effortTrend` uses the mean (RPE − expected) of the last 3 sessions, with ±1.5 meaning heavy or light. `fitMinutes` trims a session, but never below 10 minutes. `sessionStrain` projects how much strain a planned session adds. `bedtimeMinute(need, wake)` exists in [sleep_plan.dart](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/sleep_plan.dart). Of 11 actionable longevity levers, only `activeMinutes` and `strength` reshape the plan through `_applyFocus`. Steps, sleep duration and regularity, alcohol and breathing have only UI text in `leverHow` ([coach.dart:493-533](../../packages/scoring/lib/src/coach.dart); [longevity.dart screen:203-224](../../app/lib/src/screens/longevity.dart)). `focusProgress` already measures weekly progress for activeMinutes, strength, steps and sleepDuration ([longevity_service.dart:105-139](../../app/lib/src/core/longevity_service.dart)).

*Evidence (repo) — the gaps.* `PlanDays` holds `date, session, original, reason, adaptedAt, general`. It has no status, slot or `algoVersion`, even though plan rows are derived data ([tables.dart:154-164](../../packages/store/lib/src/tables.dart)). `adaptToday` returns early once `plan_adapted == today` ([coach_service.dart:133](../../app/lib/src/core/coach_service.dart)). Only guided live workouts carry a `plan` JSON. Auto-detected workouts are deleted and recreated on every rescore by `replaceAutoWorkouts` ([database.dart:365-450](../../packages/store/lib/src/database.dart)), so **a foreign key from a plan row to an auto workout would dangle**. There are two notification IDs on one default-importance channel, `tempo_daily`, with no payload, actions or tap handler. The morning call body is static ("Open Tempo near your band…") ([notifications.dart:18-115](../../app/lib/src/core/notifications.dart)). `TempoNotifications` is a concrete singleton, and no tests cover notifications ([services_test.dart](../../app/test/services_test.dart)). The Coach screen never shows the focus lever ([coach.dart screen](../../app/lib/src/screens/coach.dart)). The band's buzz through 2A06 and the Alert Notification service 0x1811/2A46 are still `TODO(verify)` or unused ([mi_band.dart:564-574](../../packages/band_ble/lib/src/mi_band.dart); [decisions.md](../../docs/decisions.md)). Finally, CLAUDE.md still says "Current phase: Phase 0", although the code is well past that phase.

*Design inference.* Two architectural choices follow. First, **persist user intent and derive completion**. Store on `plan_days` only what the user or coach decided (slot, planned minute, skipped or moved, plus who decided). Recompute "done" or "partial" by matching against `workouts` on every evaluation, because auto workouts are rebuilt on each rescore. Second, **keep `adaptToday` as the once-a-morning week adapter and add a stateless `replanToday`** that may run any number of times a day. Its output is a deterministic function of (plan row, now, today's workouts, bedtime, readiness), so running it repeatedly is safe.

## Build plan: six phases, each gated

The phases below follow CLAUDE.md's rules. Phases run in order. Every scoring rule lives in `packages/scoring` with no Flutter imports and carries unit tests. Derived rows carry `algo_version` (`coach-day-1` and `nudge-1` initially). Every decision gets a dated line in `docs/decisions.md`. No new BLE bytes are used without `// TODO(verify)` and a packet log in `docs/packets/`. All thresholds below are *design inferences* anchored to the evidence above, and each is a named constant so it can be versioned.

### Phase C0 · Groundwork and decisions (1–2 days)

Update `CLAUDE.md` "Current phase" to say Tempo Coach (this needs user sign-off, see open decisions), and add C0–C5 to `PLAN.md` → Milestones. In `app/lib/src/core/notifications.dart`, extract an interface `NotificationSink { schedule(NudgeSpec); cancel(int id); cancelAllCoach(); show(NudgeSpec); }`. Back it with a `PluginNotificationSink` over flutter_local_notifications and a `FakeNotificationSink` in `app/test/support/`. Inject it through a Riverpod provider and through a top-level setter for background isolates. Add tests that cover today's behaviour: the morning call at `morning_call`, and the bedtime nudge at `(bedtime − bedtime_nudge) % 1440`. Log the decisions in `docs/decisions.md`: "Tempo Coach = intent persisted, completion derived", "notifications are pre-scheduled 48 h ahead and fully replaced on every evaluation", and "USE_EXACT_ALARM not used". **Gate:** all existing tests pass, the new notification tests pass against the fake, and behaviour on the device is unchanged.

### Phase C1 · Completion model and schema v8

**Store** (`packages/store/lib/src/tables.dart`, `database.dart`, `schemaVersion → 8`). This phase adds nullable columns to `plan_days`:

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `slot` | text? | `am` \| `pm` \| null (any) |
| `plannedMinute` | int? | minute of day the session is planned for |
| `status` | text, default `planned` | user/coach intent: `planned`, `skipped`, `moved`, `rest_swapped` (done/partial are derived, not stored) |
| `statusSource` | text? | `user`, `notification`, `coach` |
| `statusAt` | datetime? | when intent changed |
| `algoVersion` | text? | algo that produced the row (`coach-1` for existing, `coach-day-1` new) |

It also adds a new **append-only** table, `nudge_log`, with the columns `id` (auto-increment), `ts`, `day`, `kind`, `notifId`, `fireAt`, `event` (`scheduled`, `cancelled`, `posted`, `tapped`, `action`, `expired`), `action?`, `payload` (JSON), and `algoVersion`. Add `nudge_log` to `restorable`. Check that `restoreRows` fills defaults when it restores a v7 export (per [database.dart:531-534](../../packages/store/lib/src/database.dart), unknown columns are dropped, but missing ones need defaults). Add a migration test for v7→v8 in `packages/store/test/store_test.dart`.

**Scoring.** Add a new file, `packages/scoring/lib/src/coach_day.dart`, and export it from the barrel. It contains `SessionMatch matchSession(Session planned, List<DoneWorkout> workouts)`, where `DoneWorkout` is a plain-Dart view holding `{start, end, sport, minutes, zoneMinutes, strain}`. A workout is **compatible** when the sport is the same. Any cardio sport counts for an easy or Z2 aerobic session, strength requires strength, and yoga/mobility accepts yoga or walking. Classification follows TrainingPeaks' compliance bands ([TrainingPeaks guide](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/231472468-TrainingPeaks-Athlete-User-Guide)): `done` when compatible minutes are ≥80% of planned, or day strain added is ≥ `planned.strainLo`. `partial` covers 50–80%. Otherwise the session is `notDone`. A hard session counts as `done` only with ≥5 minutes in Z4+Z5, the same rule `daysSinceHard` already uses ([coach_service.dart:87-125](../../app/lib/src/core/coach_service.dart)). Otherwise it becomes `doneEasier`. `DayStatus deriveStatus(row, match, now, slotEnd, bedtime)` returns `done | partial | doneEasier | pending | missedSlot | missedDay | skipped | moved`. `missedSlot` applies once the slot's planned minute plus duration plus a 120-minute grace period has passed with no match. `missedDay` applies after bedtime − 60.

**App.** `loadToday` ([today.dart](../../app/lib/src/core/today.dart)) computes `TodayData.status` with `matchSession`. Today's and Coach's `WorkoutCard` show a status chip (Planned 07:00 · Done ✓ · Partial · Missed). The overflow menu offers "Mark done", "Skip today" and "Move to evening". These write intent through a new `CoachService.setIntent(day, status, {slot, minute, source})`, which follows the `_replace` pattern ([workout_detail.dart:24-41](../../app/lib/src/screens/workout_detail.dart)). **Tests:** `coach_day_test.dart` cases cover sport compatibility, the 80/50 bands, a hard session without Z4+, auto workouts rebuilt between evaluations, and a manual skip overriding a match. A screens test renders the status chips in both themes. **Gate:** for 7 consecutive real days on the device, the derived statuses match what the user actually did, checked by hand against the Workouts list, and the v7→v8 migration preserves existing rows and restores.

### Phase C2 · Intra-day replanning and readiness overrides (pure Dart)

**Scoring** (`coach_day.dart`, `algo_version: coach-day-1`). The rule table below maps each rule to its basis.

| Rule | Function | Thresholds (defaults) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|

Fold `rhrFlag` and `shortSleep` into `dayState` behind new optional parameters. The defaults must keep today's behaviour, so existing tests stay green. Once a day the existing `adaptWeek` consumes yesterday's `missedDay` key session through `coach_carried`. That changes `CoachService.adaptToday` only to call `onMissedDay(yesterday)` before it adapts. `replanToday` runs on every `loadToday` and is never persisted except as user intent.

**Settings keys** (in [profile.dart](../../app/lib/src/core/profile.dart) `Keys`): `coach_slot` (`am`\|`pm`\|`flex`, defaulting to the median start time of confirmed or live workouts over 28 days, else `pm`), `coach_slot_am` (default `07:00`) and `coach_slot_pm` (default `18:00`). **App:** when `replanToday` returns a rescue, the session card shows "Missed this morning → 18:00 easy 30′ (bedtime 22:45)" with the actions "Plan it", "Shorter" and "Skip". **Tests:** a table-driven `replanToday` test runs bedtime 22:30 with now ∈ {10:00, 17:00, 19:30, 21:45} × {easy, hard} and checks the expected tier. Further tests cover the `rhrFlag` boundaries (4.9 vs 5.0 bpm, 3-day run, fewer than 21 baseline days → no flag), monotony with zero-SD weeks (it must not divide by zero), and carry-versus-drop by key type. An app services test checks that a missed hard Tuesday becomes `coach_carried` and lands on Thursday. **Gate:** all rule tests pass. A scripted replay over the user's real last 60 days (a new test in `tools/backtest` or `app/test`) shows how often each override would have fired, and the user accepts the rate (for example, `illness` on ≤ 3 days in 60 unless the user was actually ill).

### Phase C3 · One Coach screen: plan plus longevity actions

**Scoring.** `List<LeverAction> leverActions(Lever focus, FocusProgress p, DayContext d)` lives in `coach_day.dart`. It turns the focus lever into at most two actions for the day. **Steps:** the daily target comes from `leverTarget` (10k, or 8k at 60+), and the action is "walk 10′ after lunch" when the pace at 15:00 is below 0.6 × target × fraction of waking day elapsed. **activeMinutes:** the weekly remainder divided by days left. **Strength:** "session N of 2", which never lands on a hard-cardio day. **sleepDuration/sleepRegularity:** "in bed by HH:MM" from `bedtimeMinute`, plus the ±30-minute window. **Alcohol:** an evening check-in through Journal, with no push. The remaining levers return nothing.

**UI.** `app/lib/src/screens/coach.dart` becomes "Tempo Coach", top to bottom:

1. The readiness line with a one-line why, for example "RHR +6 bpm vs your usual → easy day".
2. Today's session card with status and rescue.
3. An "Also today" list of lever actions with checkmarks derived from data.
4. This week's `WeekBars` with status glyphs.
5. A cardio "load trend" card.
6. A compact focus card that links to Longevity.

The Longevity screen keeps Tempo Age, the lever cards and "Make this my focus". `_FocusCard` moves or is shared. Today's `WorkoutCard` keeps the status chip so the two tabs agree. **Tests:** screens_test covers Coach in each state (rest, go, missed with rescue, done, paused, calibrating, realign card) in both themes and at large text, and the existing Longevity tests keep passing. **Gate:** the user has run the merged screen for a week and confirms the Coach tab alone answers "what do I do today?" without opening Longevity.

### Phase C4 · Notification engine v2 with actions

**Scoring.** A new file, `packages/scoring/lib/src/nudges.dart` (pure; `algo_version: nudge-1`), provides `List<NudgeSpec> planNudges(NudgeContext ctx)`. `NudgeContext` holds now, wake, bedtime, slots, today's plan, status, rescue, lever actions, last-sync age, live-session flag, prefs, and nudge-log counts for the past 24 h and 7 d. The function returns specs `{kind, id, fireAt, title, body, actions, channel, deepLink}` for the next 48 h, after applying quiet hours, caps, spacing and backoff. Because the function is pure, every cap and timing rule can be unit-tested without the plugin.

**Notification inventory** (all copy is a sample; titles ≤30 characters, bodies ≤40):

These global rules apply on top of the table. Nothing fires inside the quiet window (bedtime → wake, learned from `sleep_plan` and editable) or during a live session. Interruptive pushes (DEFAULT) are at least 90 minutes apart. Each day allows at most 1 proactive nudge (`session`/`missed`/`lever`), at most 4 in total and at most 14 non-requested pushes a week. When a category is ignored 3 times in a row (no tap or action within 2 h), it fires every other day. After 5 ignores it pauses for 7 days and an in-app card is shown. "Not today" and "Skip" reset the counter. Pause mode suppresses everything except `winddown`. On Android, lock-screen visibility is PRIVATE with a public version that hides numbers, and iOS uses `hiddenPreviewsBodyPlaceholder`.

**Action handling.** `onDidReceiveNotificationResponse` handles foreground taps. A top-level `@pragma('vm:entry-point') notificationActionBackground` opens `openDb()`, applies the intent (`setIntent`, `saveRpe`, `setPause`), appends a row to `nudge_log`, reruns `planNudges` and re-issues the schedule. **Settings keys:** `notif_cats` (a JSON map of kind → 0/1; defaults on except `weekly`), `notif_cap` (proactive nudges a day, 0–2, default 1), `notif_precise` (`0`/`1`, Android only), `notif_backoff` (JSON kind → `{ignored, pausedUntil}`; derivable from `nudge_log` but cached here), and `notif_v2_migrated` (deletes the legacy `tempo_daily` channel and cancels IDs 1 and 2). ### Phase C5 · Learning when to nudge (optional)

Add `SlotModel` to `nudges.dart`: a Beta–Bernoulli Thompson sampler per (kind × slot bucket × weekday/weekend), with prior Beta(2,2). Outcomes are 1 for an action within 2 h, 0.5 for a tap only and 0 for ignored. At least 15% of the time it picks a random allowed slot, so the model keeps learning. It changes the timing of `lever` and `session` reminders only within the user's allowed windows, never the morning brief or wind-down. Decisions are logged to `nudge_log` with `algo_version: nudge-2`. **Tests:** sampler determinism under a seeded RNG, posterior updates, and exploration clipping. **Gate:** 4 weeks of `nudge_log` show the act rate on adaptive slots is no worse than on the fixed slots of the previous 4 weeks. If it is worse, roll back to `nudge-1`.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that Tempo's adaptive coach is mostly a *time and intent* problem, not a modelling problem. The repo's pure planner already makes defensible daily choices. What's missing is the ability to know at 10:00 that the 07:00 run didn't happen, to know when the user goes to bed, and to ask the user in one tap rather than guess. Two design choices remove most of the risk. Persisting intent while deriving completion copes with auto-workouts being rebuilt. Writing notification copy as a question that its own actions answer copes with a sync that iOS or a Xiaomi battery manager may never run.

The evidence is strongest exactly where Tempo plans to be strict: no stacking, binary downgrades, and the 4-hour bedtime buffer. It is weakest where Tempo has to rely on its own signals: RHR-only readiness from a band with no RR intervals, and backoff thresholds that nobody has published. That argues for shipping the RHR and nudge rules as versioned, logged and backtested defaults (`coach-day-1`, `nudge-1`), treating the user's own `nudge_log` and plan statuses as the dataset that tunes them, and keeping the default push volume lower than any of the commercial products studied.
